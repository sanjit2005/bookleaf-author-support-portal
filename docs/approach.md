# BookLeaf Support Portal — approach

**Priority.** I prioritized the complete author-to-operations support loop and AI assistance: authors see their own books and royalties, raise book-specific or general queries, and receive replies without refreshing. Admins triage by urgency, age and filters, manage ownership/status, and keep private notes separate. Original business data is preserved rather than “corrected” to fit an invented schema.

**Architecture.** React, Vite and Tailwind provide a responsive JavaScript interface. Express ES modules separate authentication, validation, services, persistence and serialization. MongoDB/Mongoose references connect users, authors, books and tickets; public replies and internal notes have separate collections and DTOs. bcrypt protects passwords; short-lived JWTs stay in browser memory. Backend role checks and ownership-scoped queries enforce access. Transactions keep ticket activity and messages/notes atomic; revision preconditions reject stale management edits. Decimal128 money serializes as strings, and date-only strings avoid timezone shifts. The explicit insert-or-compare seed preserves all 10 authors and 18 books and refuses conflicts.

**AI.** Backend-only Gemini Developer API requests use strict category/priority schemas and a configurable low-cost model (default gemini-3.5-flash-lite, documented with a free tier). System instructions combine BookLeaf's supplied policy with minimal ownership-verified book facts; ticket text and public conversation are untrusted data. Nulls remain unavailable, and unsupported payout dates, actions and royalty breakdowns must not be invented. Classification failure preserves ticket creation and marks manual review. One initial draft is attempted per eligible admin ticket opening; local edits take precedence. Regeneration is explicit, and every AI draft requires human review and a separate send. Input/history/output limits, deadlines, process-local budgets and no automatic retries bound cost. Private notes and credentials never enter AI context.

**Trade-offs and evidence.** Polling every 20 seconds is simpler than sockets; editing pauses updates, while independent cursor histories retain older conversations. Sessions and unsent work are memory-only. Attachments are selection-only, with an honest no-upload notice. Mocked API/browser tests and offline source checks establish contracts and regressions, not live model accuracy, real database concurrency or deployed behavior. Two synthetic live Gemini smoke requests passed, but broader AI evaluation and deployment remain outstanding; no successful public demo is claimed.

**Production evolution.** Verify actual hosting proxy/CORS behavior, run a small approved model-quality evaluation, and complete live role/workflow checks. Then add shared rate/cost limits, observability with redaction, measured query/index tuning and durable audit/idempotency controls. Introduce uploads or sockets only when operational needs justify them.
